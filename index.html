[01 // INTRODUCTION]

Real Systems & Hard Lessons

I’m a Computer Science student at IIIT-Delhi. If you look at my transcript, my GPA sits at a very average 5/10. Standard exams were never my strong suit, and I’m okay admitting that. My real education didn’t happen in lecture halls, but in the quiet hours of the night—struggling with broken sockets, optimizing slow database queries, and figuring out why a production service crashed.

I build because I love the raw process of making systems work. From multi-agent pipelines (ResolveAI) to async queues (HERALD) and regional betting apps, I’ve learned that the best engineering is forged through patient, repeated failure rather than performative speed.

My Chakma roots keep me grounded. Technology isn’t just logic; it connects back to real people. Whether I'm building tools or coordinating support networks for Northeastern students migrating to Delhi through the Delhi Chakma Students' Union, I care about the human element.

To balance this focus, I rely on physical cadence—mountain biking, long-distance running, and tuning car ECUs. They remind me that real growth, like endurance, is about consistency, rhythm, and quiet effort.
